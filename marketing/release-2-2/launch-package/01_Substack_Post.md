The buddy list is the product.
Today Apple approved H.I.M. 2.2, and it is already live in the App Store.
That sentence is concise. The route here was not.
H.I.M. has always been built around a simple belief: friendship deserves software designed for friendship. Not dating with the labels changed. Not a feed you have to perform for. Not a map that quietly suggests the nearest person is the most relevant one.
Just people, conversation, and enough context to make saying hi feel natural.
Version 2.2 gets much closer to that idea.
The Buddy List is home now
The biggest change is also the easiest to explain. Your profile now lives at the top of your Buddy List.
That means the app opens on a clearer home base: you, then your people. Tap your profile card when you want to edit it. Look down the list when you want to see who is around. Move between rooms and discovery without losing the sense that H.I.M. is built around relationships that persist.
A buddy list should not feel like a contact directory. It should feel like a small map of the people who matter.
That is the product.
Better discovery, with an actual reason
Suggested Buddies can now help you find people through mutual buddies and shared rooms. Buddy photos make the list easier to recognize at a glance. When you open a profile, mutual context helps explain why that person may already feel familiar.
This matters because “here is another profile” is not much of an introduction.
Context is.
Discovery is limited to members who choose to be discoverable, and the new Privacy Controls make that choice easier to manage from the Buddy List.
Your people, sorted your way
Version 2.2 also introduces private Buddy Circles. You can group people in a way that makes sense to you, without turning friendship into a public ranking system.
Friends from a room. People nearby in spirit, if not geography. The ones you can message after six months and continue the sentence.
The labels are yours. The circles are private.
A quieter way to say hi
There is also Knock.
Knock is one tap that says, “you around?” No opening paragraph. No pressure to manufacture a topic. Just a small signal that makes it easier to begin.
You can also reply to away messages now, which gives those little status notes somewhere to go. In rooms, an aggregate seen count can show how many people caught your message without making the tally feel heavier than it is.
Useful context, lightly held.
Why this shape
Product mechanics are never neutral.
A swipe says: decide.
A proximity grid says: nearest first.
A buddy list says: this connection can still be here tomorrow.
H.I.M. borrows a little warmth from the internet many of us grew up with, but the point is not to reenact it pixel for pixel. The point is to recover something it understood: identity can start with a screenname, conversation can be the main event, and friendship does not need to pretend it is merely waiting to become something else.
Version 2.2 is not the end of that work. There will be rough edges. I will keep fixing them, listening, and making the app more useful without making it louder.
For today, though, the Buddy List is back at the center.
This time, it grew up.
The light’s on. Come say hi.
